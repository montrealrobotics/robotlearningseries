# Talk abstract
abstract: |-
  Robots are increasingly capable of seeing and reasoning about the world, yet they still struggle with the physical interactions that people perform effortlessly: grasping, using tools, and adapting when things don't go as planned. In this talk, I will present our work on robots that combine vision, touch, and learning to manipulate objects in contact-rich settings. I will discuss how tactile sensing and physics-informed models make robot policies more reliable, how large-scale pretraining and language can help robots recover from errors and generalize across tasks, and how these ideas carry over to applications in manufacturing and logistics. I will close with lessons from deploying manipulation systems at scale and the open problems that remain in closing the gap between broadly capable models and dependable robots.
